Abstract—The current approaches to classify 3D point clouds
show significant drops in their performance due to the shift in
domains because of differences in noise levels, sampling rates, and
sensor properties. Conventional approaches concentrate mainly
on learning features from individual points while ignoring the
stability of structural connections among object parts. In this
work, we propose a structure-aware approach to achieve domain
generalization based on Graph Neural Networks (GNN). Our
proposed method includes preprocessing, data augmentation,
graph generation from object parts, and contrastive alignment.
We validate our findings experimentally on ModelNet40 and
PointDA-10 datasets.
Keywords—Domain Generalization, Dynamic Graph CNN, Graph
Neural Networks, Point Cloud Learning.
I. INTRODUCTION
Classification on 3D point cloud has been an active research topic
in recent years due to its extensive use in self-driving, robotics,
augmented reality, indoor scenes analysis, and medical imaging. A
point cloud is a set of points defined by 3D coordinates obtained from
devices such as LiDAR, RGB-D, and depth sensors. Different from
traditional image data, point clouds can better represent real-world
objects and scenes in terms of geometry and spatial information.
In recent years, numerous deep neural network models have been
proposed for 3D point cloud analysis. Although these methods have
achieved impressive performance on point cloud classification, they
are trained and tested on the splits of the same dataset. However,
for real-world applications, scanning objects with LiDAR sensors,
and then performing annotation to build a sufficiently large and
representative dataset is labor-expensive, and costly. Sampling point
clouds from well-annotated CAD models, on the other hand, is
relatively faster and easier. Thus, in practice, using a synthetic CAD-
based dataset (e.g. ModelNet and ShapeNet ) as the training set
and evaluating the model on a real-world dataset (e.g. ScanNet) is a
more labor-effective and efficient way. Yet, this approach introduces
a significant domain shift challenge, since point clouds scanned
from the real world often suffer from occlusion and missing points,
whereas point clouds sampled from CAD models are very regular.
The significant divergence between the distributions of the synthetic
and real point clouds causes the performance of many existing point
cloud classification models degrade significantly [1]. Hence, it is
essential to develop a 3D point cloud classification model to mitigate
the domain shift issue. Thus, Domain Generalization has become an
important research area for developing high-quality and transferable
3D perception systems that can perform well in target domains that
are not seen during the training phase. Domain generalization is
designed to learn domain-invariant representations without having
access to parts of target-domain data during training, whereas do-
main adaptation requires partial access to target-domain data during
training. But effective domain generalization for point cloud data
is quite difficult due to the irregular structure, unordered particles
and complex spatial relationships between point clouds. Most ex-
isting methods only consider feature alignment, feature distribution
matching and neglect stable structural relationships between adjacent
object regions, leading to weak invariant feature learning ability. In
the real-world, while there can be vastly different distributions of
points in different domains, the relationship between the points of
the object may be relatively stable. For instance, a chair always has
relationships between the legs, seat and backrest, no matter how noisy
the sensor is or what light conditions are set. Likewise, the semantic
structural relations of objects like tables, sofas and cabinets remain
unchanged even with severe domain shifts. This observation paves the
way to developing robust cross-domain understanding of point clouds
by leveraging structure-aware learning.so this work is motivated by
these results, then selected the structure-aware system.
In this work is mainly focusing on domain-shift along with the
structure aware framework for 3D cloud classification is proposed
based using Graph Neural Networks. this system helps us to in-
tegrate the systems functions of preprocessing,augmentation,feature
extraction by Dynamic Graph CNN,this was followed by part-level
graph construction and contrastive learning to learn about the domain
invariant structrual representations.To model the relations between the
parts of the objects it used Graph Attention Networks which increase
the overall robustness of domainshift.
This framework is evaluated using the ModelNet40 and PointDA-
10 benchmark datasets to analyze its effectiveness under cross-

II. RELATEDWORKS
The development in the 3D point cloud classification made a
progress fast and rapidly with the some deep learning and some
geomentric representation techniques. Traditional methods mainly
focused on handcrafted geometric division of objects into segments of
parts for domain-shift generalization with statistical feature extraction
methods. The traditional methods, however, proved themselves to
be inadequate in the ability to learn complex spatial relationships
and to adapt to real-world large-scale point cloud data. Recently,
the development of deep learning has led to a family of end-to-
end learning systems that can directly process unordered point sets
and learn discriminative geometric features for classification. Existing
approaches attempt to improve robustness using feature alignment,
sequence modeling, and multi-view learning,But there still exists
some limitations.
One of the first deep learning frameworks for point cloud pro-
cessing was named PointNet. The model directly takes unordered
point sets and uses shared multilayer perceptrons (MLP) followed
by a symmetric max-pool to get permutation invariance. The ar-
chitecture showed that deep neural networks could learn point-wise
features effectively, without handcrafted descriptors. PointNet is able
to produce impressive results on benchmark datasets, but the model
was primarily designed for the extraction of global features and was
not able to capture detailed local geometric relationships between
neighboring points. Consequently, it could not be used for more
complex geometric analysis tasks.
DG-MVP introduced a multi-view based framework for 3D domain
generalization, changing the point clouds into various 2D depth
projections. The method doesn’t process the irregular point clouds
directly, but uses convolutional networks to extract features from a
number of projected views, which decreases the effect of missing
points and occlusion. Moreover, synthetic noise augmentation tech-
niques for simulating real RGB-D sensor distortions were proposed
by DG-MVP, which enhances robustness in synthetic-to-real transfer
scenarios. The method showed an improvement on the cross domain
generalization, but the use of 2D projections could result in a partial
loss of the intrinsic geometric relationships found in the original 3D
structures [1].
For long-range dependencies in point clouds, PointDGMamba
proposed to leverage generalized state-space models (SSMs) as
the domain generalization tool. The framework proposed three
key components: Masked Sequence Denoising (MSD), Sequence-
wise Cross-domain Feature Aggregation (SCFA) and Dual-level
Domain Scanning (DDS). The whole system enhanced global re-
ceptive field learning without increasing the computational com-
plexity. PointDGMamba outperformed the conventional convolutional
and transformer-based approaches in modeling sequential geometric
representation to enhance robustness on unseen geometric domains.
Yet, although it is efficient, the framework still has challenges in
maintaining the fine-grained geometric consistency at the local level
in the presence of strong domain variations [2].
Based on the sequence modeling techniques, PointDGRWKV
presented the first architecture for domain generalized point cloud
classification using RWKV . The model is a hybrid of the transformer
and recurrent computation with global dependency modeling, using
linear complexity. It proposes Adaptive Geometric Token Shift (AGT-
Shift) to effectively maintain spatial geometric information and Cross-
Domain Key Distribution Alignment (CD-KDA) to alleviate the at-
tention drift due to domain discrepancies. PointDGRWKV enhanced
the generalization performance while minimizing the computational
burden with the efficient sequence learning power of RWKV . How-
ever, the approach remains largely sensitive to the success of the
token ordering strategies and can suffer from restrictions if used with
spatially very irregular distributions [3].
The other direction of domain generalization that is important
to consider is adaptive expert learning. To address this challenge,
Point-MoE proposed a novel Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) approach that
specialized features to improve generalization over various domains.
The framework can dynamically route the point cloud features
through the specific expert subnetworks, allowing the system to learn
domain-invariant representations while maintaining the discriminative
geometric information. The expert-routing strategy also enhances
scalability and robustness for the heterogeneous point cloud distri-
butions. But, training complexity can increase and expert imbalance
problems during optimization can arise in MoE based architectures
[4].
Another approach that has proven to be effective for enhancing
domain robustness is prompt-based adaptation. Point-PRC proposed
a fast regulation framework which integrates learnable prompts into
the feature learning process for point clouds. The method controls
feature representations by using domain-aware prompts to foster uni-
formity in semantic understanding across domains. The combination
of prompt learning and domain generalization tasks enabled Point-
PRC to be more adaptable and robust without needing target-domain
supervision. Although these benefits exist, prompt-based methods
could be sensitive to prompt initialization and tuning strategies,
impacting the generalization and stability of their performance on
highly diverse datasets [5].
While large strides have been made recently in cross-domain
Point Cloud Classification tasks, there are some key drawbacks.
Many current methods consider mostly learn by projection, sequence
modelling, feature alignment and/or prompt optimization and pays
less focus on explicit structure-aware graph learning and stable mod-
elling of neighbourhood relationships. In addition, many frameworks
include high computational complexity and do not have explainability
mechanisms for interpreting the learned geometric representations.
In addition, the very few methods to combine structural learning
techniques on the graph, supervised contrastive learning, attention
and explainable AI in a single mechanism for domain generalization
are just recently introduced. Given these drawbacks, we propose a
new Structure-Aware Domain Generalization framework on 3D point
cloud classification in cross-domain settings, which integrates the
Dynamic Graph CNN (DGCNN), neighborhood learning based on
graphs, attention pooling, supervised contrastive learning, domain
consistency regularization, and explainability [6].
III. METHODOLOGY
The proposed work presents a new Structure-Aware Domain Gen-
eralization framework for classification of 3D point cloud via graph-
based deep learning approach. The primary focus of the framework
is to enhance the robustness of the point cloud classification models,
under domain shift conditions, by learning domain-invariant structural
representations, from the geometric relationship between the neigh-
boring points. In contrast to the traditional one, which has concen-
trated on feature alignment and global feature extraction, the proposed
approach has focused on structure-aware learning and modelling the
relationships between neighbourhood features for enhancing the cross
domain generalization.
The overall system involves several stages point cloud prepro-
cessing, data augmentation, feature extraction using a graph-based
approach, generation of embeddings, supervised contrastive learning,
and classification. First, the raw point cloud data are normalized and
uniformly sampled to achieve consistency among various domains.
Local geometric relationships among neighboring points are then

modeled by constructing dynamic graphs with the k-Nearest Neigh-
bor (k-NN) algorithm. The point cloud data is processed into these
graph structures to extract both the local and the global geometric
features by means of Dynamic Graph CNN (DGCNN).Supervised
contrastive learning is used on the generated embeddings for more
domain-invariant feature learning. The contrastive learning mech-
anism ensures that samples of the same class are more similar
in the embedding space and the difference between the classes is
greater. This helps the model to be more robust and generalize under
new domain conditions. Finally, fully connected classification layers
are used to classify the final object category using the optimized
embeddings.
The framework is tested with the ModelNet40 benchmark datasets
and PointDA-10 benchmark datasets, in the cross domain clas-
sification scenario. The effectiveness of the proposed framework
is evaluated by various evaluation metrics such as classification
accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score, confusion matrix analysis and
embedding visualization. The implementation is done using PyTorch
with support of GPU acceleration for efficient large-scale training
and evaluation.

A. Inputs and Outputs
The input to the proposed system is a 3D point cloud which is
a set of unordered points that hold spatial coordinate information
as (x,y,z). The point cloud data used are from benchmark data sets
like ModelNet40 or PointDA-10, where many object classes and
data from various domains have been collected. Each sample is
represented by a constant number of points, sampled evenly from
the original representation of the object. The input point cloud goes
through several steps such as preprocessing, graph construction,
feature extraction, embedding generation, and classification. To model
the local geometric relationships between points, the neighbouring
points are identified during graph construction by applying the k-
Nearest Neighbor algorithm. The system output is the predicted label

not only yields classification results, but also optimized embedding
representations which can be visualized for domain separation and
feature clustering behavior analysis. Additionally, outputs on per-
formance evaluation like an accuracy score, confusion matrix, and
embedding visualizations are also generated to evaluate the robustness
and effectiveness of the framework.
B. Pre-processing
The main reason for pre-processing is that there are scale, density,
orientation, and noise variations in the raw point cloud data collected
from different domains in the proposed Structure-Aware Domain
Generalization framework. These inconsistencies may diminish the
performance of the model and the ability to generalise. So, data is
preprocessed to normalize data and enhance the geometric feature
learning.
Firstly, raw point clouds for datasets like ModelNet40 and
PointDA-10 are loaded. Point cloud data is made up of unordered 3D
coordinates (x, y, z). All objects are then subsequently normalized
by removing the mean of the objects and scaling them to a unit
sphere, making the objects have consistent spatial representation
between all samples. Then, point sampling is done to ensure an
equal number of points per object, which lowers the amount of
computation required, while still retaining the necessary geometric in-
formation. Data augmentation is used during training with techniques
like random rotation, scaling, translation and jittering to provide
robustness and avoid overfitting. Dynamic graph construction is done
after augmentation by the k-Nearest Neighbor (k-NN) algorithm,
where the points adjacent to each other are connected by Euclidean
distance. This graph structure reflects local geometric relationships
and facilitates good structural feature learning. Last but not least,

the DGCNN based feature extraction module for further training.
In summary, preprocessing enhances the consistency, robustness and
cross domain generalization performance of data.

IV. EXPERIMENTALEVALUATION
The experimental evaluation section provides an analysis on the
effectiveness of the proposed Structure-Aware Domain Generalization
approach in a cross-domain 3D point cloud classification task. The
experiments aim at assessing the framework’s capacity to acquire
from synthetic and real datasets strong and transferable geometric
representations. The proposed framework combines Dynamic Graph
CNN (DGCNN), graph-based neighborhood learning and supervised
contrastive learning to achieve better domain invariant feature learn-
ing for 3D point cloud classification problems.
Evaluation is done on benchmark point cloud datasets such as
ModelNet40, ShapeNet and ScanNet. There are domain differences
with regard to geometry, noise, density, and structural complexity in
these datasets which can be used to assess the ability to generalize
across domains. The performance and robustness of the framework
are evaluated by different evaluation metrics such as classification
accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score, confusion matrix analysis, and
embedding visualization.
A. Dataset Description
The proposed framework is tested on three popular 3D point cloud
benchmark datasets: ModelNet40, ShapeNet and ScanNet. These
datasets cover a wide range of evaluation scenarios, covering cross-
domain conditions including both synthetic CAD-based objects and
a real-world scanned environment. The ModelNet40 is a synthetic
CAD-based dataset of 12311 3D models, categorized into 40 classes
including chairs, tables, sofas, lamps, and airplanes. It is a set
of clean, uniformly sampled and noise free point cloud data and
is commonly used as a benchmark for point cloud classification
algorithms. ShapeNet is a large-scale synthetic shape database with a
large number of object classes and complex geometric structures, as
well as more intra-class variations. ShapeNet offers greater structural
diversity and greater geometric complexity than ModelNet40, making
it more difficult to learn generalizations. ScanNet is a realistic indoor
scene database gathered with RGB-D sensors. In contrast to synthetic
CAD data, the point clouds in ScanNet are noisy, have missing areas,
may be occluded, have non-uniform density and sensor artefacts.
ScanNet is very appropriate for domain generalization evaluation as
the large domain difference between the synthetic datasets and real-
world scans.
In the preprocessing phase, all the point clouds are scaled to a unit
sphere and centered on the origin. A maximum of 1024 points are
distributed in a uniform manner for each representation of the object.
To enhance robustness and reduce overfitting, data is enhanced with
random rotation, scaling, translation, and Gaussian jittering during the

the real-world scanned point clouds are noisier, occluded, incomplete

B. Evaluation Metrics
Standard classification metrics are used to evaluate the per-
formance of the proposed Structure-Aware Domain Generalization
framework: Accuracy, Precision, Recall and F1-Score. These are
the metrics for evaluating the robustness, classification ability and
domain generalization ability of the framework under the cross-
domain point cloud classification mode.Accuracy is the percentage
of all point cloud samples classified correctly, by all categories of
objects. Precision is the ratio of true positive samples to all positive
samples that are predicted; Recall measures the ratio of true positive
samples to all actual positive samples. The F1-Score is the trade-off
between Precision and Recall and is a comprehensive measure of
classification performance.

V. CONCLUSIONS ANDFUTURE WORKS
A. Conclusion
A structure-aware domain generalization framework using Dy-
namic Graph CNN (DGCNN) and supervised contrastive learning
is proposed to classify 3D point clouds. The framework focuses on
learning geometric structures to capture local relationships and extract
domain-invariant structure representations from point clouds. Exper-
